HF Workbench – Glossary

This glossary explains common terms used throughout HF Workbench and in HF antenna design.

Antenna Tuner (ATU)
A device that matches the impedance of the antenna system to the transceiver, allowing efficient power transfer even when the antenna is not resonant on the operating frequency.
Bandwidth
The range of frequencies over which an antenna presents an acceptable match (or usable performance).
Coax (Coaxial Cable)
A common type of transmission line used to connect radios to antennas. Examples include RG-8, RG-213, RG-58, etc.
Doublet
A center-fed wire antenna, typically used with balanced feedline and a tuner for multi-band operation.
DX
Long-distance communication, usually referring to contacts outside your own country or continent.
Efficiency
A measure of how much of the power delivered to the antenna is actually radiated, as opposed to being lost as heat.
Feedline
The transmission line (coax, ladder line, etc.) that carries RF energy between the radio and the antenna.
Feedpoint
The point on the antenna where the feedline connects and RF power is applied.
Ground System
For vertical antennas, the combination of radials, ground rods, and soil that completes the RF return path.
Impedance
The combination of resistance and reactance seen at the feedpoint or along the feedline, usually expressed in ohms (Ω).
Ladder Line
A type of balanced transmission line (often 300–600 Ω) used with doublets and other balanced antennas.
Loop Antenna
An antenna formed by a closed loop of wire. Small loops are often used for receive or compact installations; large loops (skyloops) can be used for multi-band operation.
NVIS (Near-Vertical Incidence Skywave)
A propagation mode where signals are radiated nearly straight up and reflected back down to cover a regional area (often a few hundred miles).
Perimeter (Loop)
The total length of wire forming a loop antenna.
Radials
Wires laid on or buried just under the ground (or elevated) that act as a return path for vertical antennas, improving efficiency and reducing ground losses.
Resonance
The condition where the reactive components of impedance cancel, leaving mostly resistive impedance at a given frequency.
Skyloop
A large horizontal loop antenna, often used for multi-band HF operation with a tuner.
Takeoff Angle
The elevation angle at which most of the antenna’s energy is radiated. Lower angles favor DX; higher angles favor NVIS/regional coverage.
Vertical Antenna
An antenna whose main radiating element is oriented vertically, often used for omnidirectional coverage and DX.
VSWR (SWR)
Voltage Standing Wave Ratio, a measure of how well the antenna system is matched to the feedline and transmitter. Lower SWR generally indicates a better match.
