HF Workbench – Quick Start

1. First-Time Setup

  1. Open your browser and navigate to the HF Workbench URL.
  2. Confirm you see the top navigation bar with Home, Doublet, Loop, Skyloop, Verticals, and Help.
  3. If the page looks “stuck,” press Ctrl+F5 (or hard refresh) to clear cached scripts.

2. A 5-Minute Tour

2.1 Home Screen

Click Home in the top navigation. You’ll see a simple welcome message and a reminder to select a tool from the navigation bar.

2.2 Opening a Tool

  1. Click Doublet to open the Doublet Designer.
  2. Wait for the “Loading…” message to be replaced by the tool interface.
  3. Repeat with Loop, Skyloop, or any of the tools under Verticals ▼.

3. Typical Use Cases

3.1 Designing a Simple 80m Doublet

  1. Click Doublet.
  2. Enter your primary band (e.g., 3.5–4.0 MHz / 80m).
  3. Set a total length (for example, around 130–140 feet for a classic 80m doublet).
  4. Enter your planned average height above ground.
  5. Review the suggested behavior on other bands (40m, 20m, etc.).
Tip: The Doublet Designer is most powerful when you’re planning a “one antenna, many bands” station using a tuner and balanced feedline.

3.2 Sketching a Skyloop

  1. Click Skyloop.
  2. Enter the approximate perimeter you can fit in your yard (e.g., 260–300 feet).
  3. Set the average height (e.g., 30–40 feet).
  4. Review how the loop behaves on 80m, 40m, and higher bands.

3.3 Exploring a Vertical for DX

  1. Click Verticals ▼ → Vertical DX Designer.
  2. Enter your available vertical height (e.g., 30–40 feet).
  3. Specify your radial system (number and length of radials).
  4. Choose your primary band (e.g., 40m or 20m).
  5. Review the estimated takeoff angle and efficiency hints.

4. Quick Reference: Which Tool Should I Use?

  • I want one wire that works on many bands: Use Doublet or Skyloop.
  • I have limited space and want a compact antenna: Use Loop or a short Vertical.
  • I want to focus on DX: Use Vertical DX Designer or Dominator Array.
  • I want strong regional coverage (NVIS): Use Vertical NVIS Designer or a low Skyloop.
