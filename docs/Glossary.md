# Antenna Workbench — Glossary

Short, operator‑focused definitions for terms used in the Workbench.

---

## A

**Antenna height**  
Physical height of the antenna above ground, usually in meters.

**Average ground**  
Typical soil with moderate conductivity and dielectric constant.

---

## B

**Band**  
A range of frequencies (e.g., 80m, 40m, 20m) used in amateur radio.

**Base gain (dBi)**  
The modeled gain of the antenna from geometry alone, before boosts.

**Boost**  
Any gain or loss applied on top of base gain due to environment, feedline, or special options.

---

## C

**Coax**  
Unbalanced feedline (e.g., RG‑213, LMR‑400) with characteristic impedance (usually 50Ω).

**Coax jumper**  
Short length of coax between a balanced line and tuner or radio.

---

## D

**dB (decibel)**  
Logarithmic unit expressing ratios of power or field strength.

**dBi**  
Gain in decibels relative to an isotropic radiator.

**Doublet**  
A center‑fed dipole (often longer than a single‑band dipole) used with balanced feedline and tuner.

**DX**  
Long‑distance communication, typically intercontinental.

---

## E

**Electrical height / length**  
Physical dimension expressed as a fraction of wavelength (e.g., 0.25λ, 1.0λ).

---

## F

**Feedline**  
Cable or line that carries RF from the transmitter to the antenna (coax, ladder line, etc.).

**Feedline loss**  
Power lost as heat in the feedline, usually expressed in dB.

---

## G

**Gain**  
Increase in signal strength in a given direction compared to a reference (usually isotropic).

**Ground loss**  
Loss due to imperfect ground conductivity and resistance.

**Ground screen**  
Mesh or radial screen placed under an antenna to improve ground performance.

---

## H

**HF (High Frequency)**  
3–30 MHz radio spectrum used heavily in amateur radio.

---

## L

**Ladder line**  
Balanced feedline (e.g., 300Ω, 450Ω, 600Ω) with low loss.

**Loop (Skyloop)**  
Closed wire loop antenna, often full‑wave or larger.

---

## N

**NVIS (Near Vertical Incidence Skywave)**  
Propagation mode using high takeoff angles (60–90°) to cover regional distances via high‑angle reflection.

**NVIS reflector**  
A wire grid or reflector placed below the antenna to enhance high‑angle radiation.

---

## R

**Radial**  
Wire used as part of a ground system for vertical antennas.

**Reflector (parasitic)**  
Element or structure that reflects RF to shape the pattern and increase gain in a direction.

---

## S

**Seaside gain**  
Enhanced performance when an antenna is placed near or over saltwater.

**Skyloop**  
A large horizontal loop antenna, often used for quiet, multiband HF operation.

**SWR (Standing Wave Ratio)**  
Measure of mismatch between feedline and load; lower is generally better.

---

## T

**Takeoff angle (TOA)**  
Elevation angle of the main lobe of radiation. Low TOA favors DX; high TOA favors NVIS.

**Transformer**  
Device used to match impedance between antenna/feedline and radio/tuner.

**Tuner (ATU)**  
A device that matches the impedance seen by the transmitter to a safe value.

---

## W

**Wavelength (λ)**  
Distance an RF wave travels in one cycle; related to frequency by λ = c / f.
